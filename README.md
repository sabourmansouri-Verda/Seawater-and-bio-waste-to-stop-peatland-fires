1. The Three-Phase Operational Vector


Phase 1: Marine Interception & Disinfection

The system intercepts existing municipal and agricultural sewage pipelines currently discharging untreated organic waste into marine environments. This waste stream is directed into localized, high-salinity marine mixing vaults. The deliberate introduction of concentrated seawater subjects enteric pathogens to fatal osmotic pressure, while solar-exposed shallow retention arrays utilize tropical ultraviolet (UV) radiation to achieve total biological sterilization.

Phase 2: Buffered Canal Injection

The resulting sterile, nutrient-dense liquid NPK slurry is introduced via controlled gravity channels into degraded, fire-prone peatland drainage networks. The marine carbonates (\(HCO_{3}^{-}\)) present in the mixture react with the highly acidic peat porewater, safely buffering the system's pH from an aggressive 3.0 to a stabilized 6.5. Simultaneously, the peat's natural humic and fulvic acids chemically lock down trace dietary metals into non-reactive, insoluble sediments.

Phase 3: Small-Scale Aggregation & Compost Synthesis

To generate localized economic independence, the stabilized matrix is mechanically harvested at small-scale community hubs. By blending the conditioned, buffered peat carbon with the sterilized marine organic solids, operators manufacture a highly fertile, pH-neutralized, mineral-rich organic compost. This product is stabilized for international export as a premium agricultural biostimulant.
